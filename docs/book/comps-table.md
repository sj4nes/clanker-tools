## What the shelf says

**The category is new.** Every direct comp is dated February–August 2026. There
is no established canon, which means low competition and also unproven demand.

**Two clusters, and the author's reading of them:** books that catalogue many
skills, and books that teach prompting for chat-style agents — with a blend in
between that mixes general AI skill-building with writing skills *for* AI.

**The gap, stated as a hypothesis rather than a finding:** the direct comps'
titles all promise *production* — build, ship, master, complete, portable,
beginner's guide. None promises *evaluation*. Nothing on this shelf appears to
answer **"is the skill I just wrote any good, and how would I know?"**

That gap is the book's opening if it is real. It is currently inferred from
titles, and needs one of two things before it can be asserted in the
manuscript: read the two or three closest comps, or state it in the book as a
characterisation of the category rather than a claim about specific titles.

## Differentiation sentence (draft)

> Existing books teach the SKILL.md format and how to ship a first skill
> quickly; this book helps someone who already writes skills find out whether a
> specific skill does anything — by building verification that can fail, and by
> measuring the default behaviour the skill claims to displace — working *with*
> the agent as a collaborator rather than writing at it.
