# subspace_intersection

## Type
proposition

## Statement
An arbitrary intersection of subspaces of V is a subspace. A UNION of two subspaces is a subspace if and only if one contains the other.

## Symbols
- `(U_i)_{i in I}` — an arbitrary family of subspaces, I possibly infinite

## Epistemic status
proposition  ·  field_scope: any_field

## Prerequisites (tsort edges into this node)
subspace, subspace_criterion

## Hypotheses
each U_i a subspace of V

## Proof provenance
technique: intersection: each closure is checked coordinatewise across the family. Union: if neither contains the other pick u in U\W and w in W\U; then u + w lies in neither
derives_from: subspace_criterion
lean_status: cited

## Type / well-formedness check
Well-formed for an arbitrary index set: the intersection of the empty family is V by convention, which is a subspace. The union clause is an iff, so it also records when the union IS a subspace.

## Specialization / boundary cases
- two distinct lines through the origin in R^2 intersect in {0}, a subspace, but their union is not
- the intersection of all subspaces containing S is span(S) -- this is span_is_smallest_subspace

## Hypothesis-dropped counterexamples
- **intersection_rather_than_union**: the x-axis and y-axis in R^2 are subspaces; their union contains (1,0) and (0,1) but not (1,1)

## Common misuse
- forming 'the subspace generated by U and W' as the union -- it is the SUM U + W (sum_of_subspaces)
