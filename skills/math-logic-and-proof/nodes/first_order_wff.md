# first_order_wff

## Type
definition  (epistemic status: `definition`; `constructive_grade: intuitionistic`
as syntax)

## Statement
The **first-order well-formed formulas** of `ℒ` are generated by the
`inductive_definition`:
- every `atomic_formula` is a wff;
- if `φ`, `ψ` are wffs, so are `¬φ`, `(φ ∧ ψ)`, `(φ ∨ ψ)`, `(φ → ψ)`,
  `(φ ↔ ψ)` (connective closure, inherited from `wff_syntax`);
- if `φ` is a wff and `x` a variable, `∀x φ` and `∃x φ` are wffs
  (`quantifier_syntax`);
- nothing else.

## Symbols
- `φ, ψ, χ`: first-order wffs.

## Prerequisites (tsort edges into this node)
`wff_syntax`, `atomic_formula`, `quantifier_syntax`, `inductive_definition`.

## Content
Propositional `wff_syntax` with **atoms replaced by `atomic_formula`s** and
**two binding clauses added**. It is uniquely readable (analogue of
`wff_unique_readability`), so `recursion_on_wff` and `structural_induction_wff`
extend — now with `∀x`/`∃x` cases (whose sub-formula has `x` free though bound in
the whole). This drives `free_bound_variables`, `tarski_satisfaction`,
`substitution`, `prenex_normal_form`.

## Constructive grade
`intuitionistic` — decidable inductive type. (Laws over it split classically —
`quantifier_negation`.)

## Lean status
`lean_status: cited`. Mathlib: `FirstOrder.Language.BoundedFormula` (de Bruijn
indices track binding depth). `validation/proof-checks.lean` is propositional
only.

## Type / well-formedness check
`well_formed`. Requirements: atomic formulas well-formed (arities);
quantifiers bind **variables**; connective arities. **Free vs bound** occurrences
are then well-defined (`free_bound_variables`). A **sentence** is a wff with no
free variable (`sentence`). Quantifier **rank** is a `formula_complexity`
measure.

## Specialization / boundary cases
- **quantifier-free** fragment: atomic formulas + connectives — essentially
  propositional logic over the atomic formulas as atoms.
- **prenex** wffs: all quantifiers in front (`prenex_normal_form` — every wff is
  equivalent to one).
- **∃-free / ∀-free** (Skolem / Herbrand forms): used in automated deduction —
  out of scope.
- 0-ary relation symbols + no quantifiers ⟹ `wff_syntax` (propositional logic
  as a fragment).

## Hypothesis-dropped counterexamples
- **quantify over relation/function symbols** (`∀R φ`): second-order logic — no
  complete proof system.
- **infinite conjunction** (`⋀_{n} φ_n`): `L_{ω₁,ω}` — non-compact, `ℕ`
  becomes categorically axiomatisable.
- **term where a formula belongs** (or vice versa): ill-typed.

## Common misuse
Confusing a term with a formula; second-order quantification by analogy;
treating `φ` (with `x` free) inside `∀x φ` as a sentence; forgetting the
quantifier clauses when doing structural induction (`coincidence_lemma`,
`truth_lemma_fol`).

## Related nodes (non-prerequisite)
- `extends`: `wff_syntax`.
- `built_from`: `atomic_formula`, `quantifier_syntax`.
- `carries`: structural induction / recursion with `∀`/`∃` cases.
- `feeds`: `free_bound_variables`, `sentence`, `substitution`,
  `tarski_satisfaction`, `prenex_normal_form`.
