## score_function

## fisher_information

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** I(theta) >= 0 real; equals E[U_1^2] because E[U_1] = 0 (score identity). Additivity n I(theta) over an iid sample is Var of a sum of iid. Under regularity also I(theta) = -E[d^2/dtheta^2 log f] (information_equality).
- **symbols:**
  - `I(theta)` — Fisher information, type: nonnegative real (scalar theta)
  - `U_1(theta)` — the single-observation score

- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## fisher_information_matrix

- **symbols:**

## score_identity

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An identity in theta, not an approximation. Proof: 1 = int f(x; theta) dx; differentiate both sides; if d/dtheta passes inside, 0 = int (d/dtheta f) dx = int (d/dtheta log f) f dx = E_theta[U_1(theta)]. Sum over i for the full score.
- **symbols:**
  - `U(theta)` — the score at theta
  - `E_theta` — expectation under P_theta (the SAME theta as in the score)
- **hypotheses:** interchange_derivative_integral, support_independent_of_theta

## information_equality

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An identity, per observation. Proof: differentiate the score identity int (d log f) f dx = 0 once more; product rule gives int (d^2 log f) f + int (d log f)^2 f = 0, i.e. E[d^2 log f] + E[(d log f)^2] = 0.
- **symbols:**
  - `I(theta)` — Fisher information
  - `d^2/dtheta^2 log f` — the observation's log-likelihood curvature
  - `-E[d^2 ell]` — the 'expected information'; its sample analogue -d^2 ell(theta_hat) is the OBSERVED information
- **hypotheses:** interchange_derivative_integral, log_likelihood_smooth

## natural_parameter_space

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** Convexity is by Holder's inequality. On int(H), A is C^infinity (indeed analytic) and E_eta|T|^m < inf for all m -- this is why exponential families are 'regular' with no fuss.
- **symbols:**
  - `H` — the natural parameter space, type: convex subset of R^k
  - `int(H)` — its interior, where A is analytic and all moments of T exist
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## cumulant_function

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** Convex (Holder); strictly convex in a minimal representation. Its gradient and Hessian are the first two cumulants of T -- see exponential_family_moment_identities.
- **symbols:**
  - `A(eta)` — the cumulant / log-partition function, type: convex real function on H
  - `grad A(eta)` — = E_eta[T(X)], the mean-value map
  - `Hess A(eta)` — = Cov_eta(T(X)), positive semidefinite (definite in the minimal representation)
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## mean_value_parametrization

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A reparametrization, well-posed because grad A is injective (Hess A = Cov(T) PD in the minimal representation). The Fisher information transforms as I(mu) = (Hess A)^{-1} = Cov_eta(T)^{-1}.
- **symbols:**
  - `mu` — the mean parameter, type: point of int(conv(supp T))
  - `grad A` — the mean map, a bijection on the interiors by strict convexity of A
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## exponential_family_moment_identities

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** Identities on int(H). Proof: differentiate A(eta) = log int h e^{eta.T} under the integral sign (legal on int(H) by the analyticity of A); first derivative gives the mean, second gives the covariance.
- **symbols:**
  - `grad A, Hess A` — gradient and Hessian of the cumulant function
  - `E_eta[T], Cov_eta(T)` — mean vector and covariance matrix of the sufficient statistic
- **hypotheses:** natural_parameter_space

## exponential_family_sufficient_statistic

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A sufficiency claim; it follows immediately from Neyman-Fisher factorization: the joint density is [prod h(x_i)] exp(eta . sum T(x_i) - n A(eta)), which is g(sum T(x_i); eta) times h-part.
- **symbols:**
  - `sum_i T(X_i)` — the natural sufficient statistic of the sample, type: R^k, dimension k regardless of n
  - `n` — sample size -- the KEY point: the sufficient statistic's dimension does not grow with n
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## exponential_family_completeness

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A completeness claim. Proof: E_eta[g(S)] = 0 for all eta in an open set makes the two-sided Laplace transform of g times the S-density vanish on an open set; by uniqueness of the Laplace transform g = 0 a.e.
- **symbols:**
  - `complete` — E_eta[g(sum T)] = 0 for all eta implies g = 0 a.s.
  - `full-rank / regular` — int(H) nonempty and T has no a.s. affine dependency
- **hypotheses:** natural_parameter_space

- **symbols:**

  - `P_theta(X in . | T = t)` — the conditional law of the data given the statistic
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## neyman_fisher_factorization

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An iff. The practical test for sufficiency: write the joint density and see whether theta interacts with the data only through T(x). In the dominated case this is rigorous (Halmos-Savage); the discrete case is a direct conditional-probability computation.
- **symbols:**
  - `g(t; theta)` — the theta-dependent factor, a function of the data ONLY through T
  - `h(x)` — the theta-free residual factor
- **hypotheses:** dominated_family

- **symbols:**

- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## completeness_statistic

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A property of the FAMILY of distributions of T, not of a single distribution. It says the only unbiased estimator of 0 based on T is 0 -- which forces uniqueness of unbiased estimators that are functions of T.
- **symbols:**
  - `g` — an arbitrary (measurable, or bounded) function on the range of T
  - `the family of laws of T` — must be 'rich enough' to kill every unbiased-of-zero g
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

- **symbols:**

- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## basu_theorem

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An independence conclusion. Proof: for any event B for A, P_theta(A in B) is a constant c (ancillarity); E_theta[ P(A in B | T) - c ] = 0 for all theta; completeness forces P(A in B | T) = c a.s., i.e. A _||_ T.
- **symbols:**
  - `T` — complete sufficient statistic
  - `A` — ancillary statistic
  - `T _||_ A` — statistical independence, for every theta
- **hypotheses:** completeness_statistic, sufficiency

## mean_squared_error

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** The risk function under squared-error loss L(theta, a) = (a - theta)^2. Requires a finite second moment of theta_hat. Decomposes as variance plus squared bias (next node).
- **symbols:**
  - `MSE(theta)` — mean squared error, type: nonnegative function of theta
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## mse_bias_variance_decomposition

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An exact algebraic identity, not an approximation. Proof: write theta_hat - theta = (theta_hat - E theta_hat) + (E theta_hat - theta); square; the cross term is 2 (E theta_hat - theta) E[theta_hat - E theta_hat] = 0.
- **symbols:**
  - `Var_theta(theta_hat)` — the sampling variance of the estimator
  - `b(theta) = E_theta[theta_hat] - theta` — the bias
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## unbiased_estimator

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A statement holding for ALL theta simultaneously. Unbiasedness is a convenient constraint that makes 'minimum variance' a well-posed optimality target, but it is not sacred (it can force large variance or absurd estimates).
- **symbols:**
  - `g(theta)` — the estimand (often theta itself)
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## consistency

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An asymptotic (n -> inf) property; says nothing about any fixed n. 'For every theta' matters -- an estimator can be consistent at some theta and not others.
- **symbols:**
  - `theta_hat_n` — the estimator based on a sample of size n
  - `-> in probability` — the mode of convergence (prob_conv_p)
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## asymptotic_variance

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** Defined only for sqrt(n)-asymptotically-normal estimators. It is NOT in general the limit of n Var(theta_hat_n) (that can differ or be infinite); it is the variance of the limiting law.
- **symbols:**
  - `Sigma(theta)/n` — the finite-n variance approximation used for standard errors
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## asymptotic_normality_estimator

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An asymptotic distributional statement. The centering is the true theta and the scaling is sqrt(n); other rates (n for uniform(0,theta), n^{1/3} for a mode / isotonic estimator) give non-normal limits and are excluded from 'asymptotically normal'.
- **symbols:**
  - `Sigma(theta)` — the asymptotic covariance, type: PSD matrix depending on theta
  - `-->^d` — convergence in distribution (prob_conv_d)
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## relative_efficiency

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A ratio of asymptotic variances. ARE(A, B) = c means B needs about 1/c times as many observations as A to match precision (large n).
- **symbols:**
  - `ARE` — asymptotic relative efficiency, type: positive real (may depend on theta)
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## asymptotic_efficiency

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** Ties an estimator to the information bound. Under regularity the MLE is efficient. The Hodges 'superefficient' estimator beats I^{-1} at a single point but at the cost of exploding risk nearby -- which is why the definition demands the equality for EVERY theta and modern theory uses the local-asymptotic-minimax formulation (see local_asymptotic_minimax).
- **symbols:**
  - `I(theta)^{-1}` — the inverse Fisher information -- the smallest asymptotic variance a regular estimator can have
- **hypotheses:** fisher_information_positive_definite

## umvue

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A global optimality statement within the unbiased class. It need not exist; when a complete sufficient statistic is available, Lehmann-Scheffe both constructs it and proves uniqueness.
- **symbols:**
  - `'uniformly'` — the variance domination holds for all theta at once
- **hypotheses:** unbiased_estimator

## cramer_rao_lower_bound

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A variance lower bound holding for a FIXED n (not asymptotic). Proof: Cov_theta(theta_hat, U(theta)) = 1 + b'(theta) (differentiate E[theta_hat] = theta + b under the integral sign and use E[U] = 0); then Cauchy-Schwarz gives (1 + b')^2 = Cov(theta_hat, U)^2 <= Var(theta_hat) Var(U) = Var(theta_hat) . n I(theta).
- **symbols:**
  - `n I(theta)` — the sample Fisher information
  - `b(theta)` — the bias; b'(theta) its theta-derivative
  - `1/(n I(theta))` — the information bound / 'CRLB'
- **hypotheses:** unbiased_estimator, support_independent_of_theta, interchange_derivative_integral

## rao_blackwell_theorem

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** Sufficiency is exactly what makes E[theta_hat | T] a legal estimator (theta-free). Proof of the variance drop: law of total variance, Var(theta_hat) = E[Var(theta_hat | T)] + Var(E[theta_hat | T]) >= Var(E[theta_hat | T]) = Var(theta_hat_RB). Same mean: tower property.
- **symbols:**
  - `theta_hat_RB = E[theta_hat | T]` — the Rao-Blackwellized estimator -- a statistic BECAUSE T is sufficient (the conditional expectation does not depend on theta)
  - `T` — a sufficient statistic
- **hypotheses:** sufficiency, unbiased_estimator

## lehmann_scheffe_theorem

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** Combines Rao-Blackwell (improvement by conditioning on T) with completeness (there is at most one unbiased function of T, since the difference of two would be an unbiased estimator of 0). Gives both existence and uniqueness of the UMVUE.
- **symbols:**
  - `phi(T)` — any unbiased estimator that is a function of the complete sufficient statistic
- **hypotheses:** completeness_statistic, sufficiency, unbiased_estimator

## crlb_attainment

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** The 'iff' comes from the Cauchy-Schwarz equality condition in the CRLB proof. Tightness forces score = c(theta)(theta_hat - theta), which integrates to an exponential-family log-density.
- **symbols:**
  - `the equality condition` — Cauchy-Schwarz is tight iff theta_hat - E[theta_hat] and the score are a.s. proportional
- **hypotheses:** unbiased_estimator

## method_of_moments

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A system of d equations in d unknowns. Consistency follows from the LLN (mbar_k -> m_k) plus continuity of the inverse moment map; asymptotic normality from the CLT + delta method. Generally NOT efficient.
- **symbols:**
  - `m_k(theta) = E_theta[X^k]` — the k-th population (raw) moment as a function of theta
  - `mbar_k = (1/n) sum X_i^k` — the k-th sample moment
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## plug_in_principle

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** Requires the estimand to be expressed as a functional of P. Consistency comes from P_hat_n -> P (Glivenko-Cantelli) plus continuity of T; asymptotic normality from the functional delta method when T is Hadamard-differentiable.
- **symbols:**
  - `T(.)` — a statistical functional, type: map from laws to R^k (the mean, a quantile, the variance, a correlation)
  - `P_hat_n` — the empirical measure
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## maximum_likelihood_estimator

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** Existence needs ell upper-semicontinuous and Theta compact (or ell -> -inf at the boundary) -- ra_compactness + ra_continuity. Uniqueness is separate (guaranteed if ell is strictly concave, e.g. a full-rank exponential family). The MLE is a function of any sufficient statistic.
- **symbols:**
  - `argmax` — the set of maximizers; theta_hat is any measurable selection when it is not unique
  - `ell(theta) = sum_i log f(X_i; theta)` — the log-likelihood
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## mle_score_equation

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** Fermat's stationarity condition, nothing more. It is NECESSARY, not sufficient -- a root of U can be a minimum or a saddle; and a boundary MLE need not be a root at all.
- **symbols:**
  - `U(theta_hat) = 0` — the first-order stationarity condition
- **hypotheses:** true_parameter_interior

## mle_invariance

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A closure property of the argmax under composition. For non-injective g one adopts the induced-likelihood definition and the property holds by construction.
- **symbols:**
  - `g` — an arbitrary function of the parameter (need not be one-to-one)
  - `L*(eta) = sup_{theta: g(theta) = eta} L(theta)` — the induced likelihood, whose maximizer is g(theta_hat)
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## mle_consistency

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An asymptotic guarantee. Wald's argument: (1/n) ell(theta) -> -K(theta_0, theta) + const uniformly; the limit is uniquely maximized at theta_0 (identifiability); so its argmax theta_hat_n -> theta_0. The 'uniformly' step needs a compactness / bracketing condition and is where proofs differ.
- **symbols:**
  - `theta_0` — the true parameter
  - `the Kullback-Leibler divergence K(theta_0, theta)` — which identifiability makes uniquely zero at theta = theta_0
- **hypotheses:** identifiability

## mle_asymptotic_normality

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An asymptotic distributional result. Proof: Taylor-expand 0 = U(theta_hat) about theta_0: 0 = U(theta_0) + U'(theta_bar)(theta_hat - theta_0). Then sqrt(n)(theta_hat - theta_0) = [-(1/n) U'(theta_bar)]^{-1} [(1/n^{1/2}) U(theta_0)]. Numerator -> N(0, I(theta_0)) by the CLT (score identity + information equality give mean 0, variance I). Denominator -> I(theta_0) by the LLN. Slutsky assembles N(0, I^{-1} I I^{-1}) = N(0, I^{-1}).
- **symbols:**
  - `I(theta_0)^{-1}` — the inverse per-observation Fisher information -- the Cramer-Rao asymptotic variance
  - `the sample version` — n(theta_hat - theta_0) approx N(0, (n I)^{-1}); SE_j = sqrt((n I(theta_hat))^{-1}_jj)
- **hypotheses:** true_parameter_interior, fisher_information_positive_definite, log_likelihood_smooth, identifiability

## m_estimator

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** Generalizes the MLE by decoupling the criterion from the true density. Under E_theta[psi(X; theta)] = 0 (the estimating equation is 'unbiased') the estimator is consistent for that theta; asymptotic normality with the SANDWICH covariance A^{-1} B A^{-T}, A = -E[grad psi], B = E[psi psi^T].
- **symbols:**
  - `m(x; theta)` — the per-observation criterion (log-density, negative loss, ...)
  - `psi(x; theta) = grad_theta m` — the estimating function / influence-defining function
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## sandwich_variance

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A covariance formula, asymptotic. The reduction A^{-1} B A^{-1} -> I^{-1} needs the information equality (correct model). Under misspecification A != B and the sandwich is the only correct large-sample variance.
- **symbols:**
  - `the 'bread' A^{-1}` — from the derivative of the estimating equation
  - `the 'meat' B` — the variance of the estimating function
  - `the estimated version` — A_hat = -(1/n) sum grad psi(X_i; theta_hat), B_hat = (1/n) sum psi psi^T at theta_hat -- the Huber-White / robust standard error
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## bayes_estimator

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An argmin over actions of the posterior risk. Well-posed whenever the posterior exists (proper, or improper with a finite normalizer) and the posterior loss is finite. It is automatically a function of a sufficient statistic.
- **symbols:**
  - `pi(theta)` — the prior density on Theta (a modeling input)
  - `pi(theta | x) proportional to L(x; theta) pi(theta)` — the posterior
  - `delta_pi` — the Bayes rule / Bayes estimator
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## posterior_mean_rule

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An argmin computed by completing the square: E[(theta - a)^2 | x] = Var(theta | x) + (E[theta | x] - a)^2, minimized at a = E[theta | x]. Same algebra as the bias-variance decomposition and the L^2-projection property of conditional expectation.
- **symbols:**
  - `E[theta | x]` — the mean of the posterior distribution
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## posterior_median_rule

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An argmin: d/da E[|theta - a| | x] = P(theta < a | x) - P(theta > a | x), which changes sign at the median. The median minimizes expected absolute deviation -- the L^1 analogue of the mean minimizing expected squared deviation.
- **symbols:**
  - `m` — a posterior median (an interval of minimizers if the posterior has a flat spot at 1/2)
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## conjugate_prior

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A closure property of a prior family under Bayesian updating for a given likelihood. Every exponential family has a conjugate prior (the exp-family prior on the natural parameter with T-plus-count hyperparameters).
- **symbols:**
  - `F` — the conjugate family (Beta, Gamma, Normal-inverse-Gamma, Dirichlet, ...)
  - `hyperparameters` — the parameters of the prior, updated by simple sufficient-statistic arithmetic
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## credible_interval

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A posterior-probability statement, conditional on the observed x and the prior. Fundamentally different from a confidence interval: here theta is random (posterior) and the interval is fixed given x; there theta is fixed and the interval is random. They coincide asymptotically for regular models (bernstein_von_mises).
- **symbols:**
  - `pi(theta | x)` — the posterior density
  - `HPD region` — the shortest such set for a unimodal posterior
  - `1 - alpha` — a posterior probability -- a statement about theta given THIS data and the prior
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## bernstein_von_mises

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A convergence-of-measures statement about the posterior, holding in P_{theta_0}-probability (a frequentist statement about a Bayesian object). The prior washes out at rate 1/sqrt(n).
- **symbols:**
  - `pi(. | X)` — the posterior
  - `TV` — total variation distance
  - `consequence` — a 95% credible interval is asymptotically a 95% confidence interval

## decision_rule

## admissibility

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A minimal-competence criterion: an inadmissible rule can be strictly improved everywhere at no cost, so it should not be used. Admissibility does NOT single out a good rule (silly rules like delta = c are often admissible).
- **symbols:**
  - `'delta' dominates delta'` — R(., delta') <= R(., delta) with strict somewhere -- a free lunch

## minimax_rule

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** The rule with the best guarantee against an adversarial Nature. Often found as a Bayes rule against a 'least favorable' prior (the prior that makes the Bayes risk largest); minimax = Bayes vs least-favorable prior when a saddle point exists.
- **symbols:**
  - `sup_theta R(theta, delta)` — the maximum risk of delta -- its worst case over Theta
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## bayes_risk

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A scalar summary of the risk function against a weighting pi. Minimizing it is what makes the Bayes rule well-defined and unique (a.s.). Different priors give different orderings.
- **symbols:**
  - `pi` — the prior (a probability measure on Theta, or an improper measure with finite r)
  - `r(pi, delta)` — a single number -- so Bayes risk TOTALLY orders the rules (unlike the risk function)

## bayes_rule_minimizes_bayes_risk

- **status:** `well_formed`

- **symbols:**
  - `the pointwise-vs-global equivalence` — minimize under the integral sign

## complete_class

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A structural result reducing the search for a good rule to the Bayes rules. The precise hypotheses (compact Theta, continuous risk, convex loss -- Wald / Le Cam) are technical; this capsule states the theorem and cites the proof.
- **symbols:**

## james_stein

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A domination statement (a counterexample to admissibility of the MLE). Proof: Stein's unbiased risk estimate (SURE) gives E||theta_hat_JS - theta||^2 = d - E[(d-2)^2/||X||^2] < d. The (d-2) requires d >= 3 for the correction to help.
- **symbols:**
  - `theta_hat_JS` — the James-Stein shrinkage estimator
  - `(d - 2)/||X||^2` — the data-driven shrinkage factor -- shrinks X toward 0 (or toward any fixed point, or a subspace)
  - `d` — the dimension; the effect is absent for d <= 2
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## chi_squared_distribution

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A construction from math-probability's Gamma and standard normal, NOT a cited primitive. Well-defined because Gamma(k/2, 1/2) is a genuine law for every k > 0, and the sum-of-squares equivalence is a change-of-variables computation (Z^2 ~ Gamma(1/2, 1/2), then Gamma shape-additivity).
- **symbols:**
  - `k` — degrees of freedom, type: positive real (integer in the sum-of-squares view)
  - `chi^2_k` — the distribution; density x^{k/2 - 1} e^{-x/2} / (2^{k/2} Gamma(k/2)) on x > 0
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## students_t_distribution

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A construction (ratio of independent N(0,1) and sqrt(chi^2_k / k)). Well-defined for k > 0. The independence of numerator and denominator is essential -- it is what Basu's theorem supplies for the one-sample t-statistic.
- **symbols:**
  - `k` — degrees of freedom
  - `t_k` — the distribution; density proportional to (1 + t^2/k)^{-(k+1)/2}
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## f_distribution

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A construction (ratio of two independent scaled chi-squares). Well-defined for d1, d2 > 0. If T ~ t_k then T^2 ~ F_{1,k}. 1/F_{d1,d2} ~ F_{d2,d1}.
- **symbols:**
  - `d1, d2` — numerator and denominator degrees of freedom
  - `F_{d1,d2}` — the distribution on (0, inf)
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## chi_squared_additivity

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An identity of laws. Immediate from chi^2_k = Gamma(k/2, 1/2) and the fact that independent Gammas with a common rate add in the shape parameter (MGF (1 - 2t)^{-a/2} (1 - 2t)^{-b/2} = (1 - 2t)^{-(a+b)/2}).
- **symbols:**
  - `a, b` — degrees of freedom of the summands
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## sample_mean

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A statistic (no theta). E_theta[Xbar] = E_theta[X_1] (linearity), Var_theta(Xbar) = Var_theta(X_1)/n (iid). Sufficient for the mean in a normal or exponential-family location model.
- **symbols:**
  - `Xbar_n` — the sample mean, type: statistic valued in R (or R^k)
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## sample_variance

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A statistic. The n - 1 comes from one degree of freedom being spent estimating the mean: sum (X_i - Xbar)^2 has only n - 1 free deviations (they sum to 0).
- **symbols:**
  - `S^2` — the sample variance, type: nonnegative statistic
  - `the n - 1 divisor` — 'Bessel's correction' -- makes S^2 unbiased for sigma^2 (bias_of_sample_variance)
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## bias_of_sample_variance

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An exact identity for every finite-variance law. Proof: sum (X_i - Xbar)^2 = sum (X_i - mu)^2 - n(Xbar - mu)^2; take expectations: n sigma^2 - n . sigma^2/n = (n - 1) sigma^2.
- **symbols:**
  - `the (n-1)` — the count of independent deviations -- n residuals constrained by sum (X_i - Xbar) = 0
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## normal_sample_mean_variance_independence

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An independence conclusion, exact and finite-sample. Three routes: (a) Basu -- Xbar is complete sufficient for mu (sigma^2 known), S^2 is ancillary for mu, so independent; (b) the orthogonal transformation of (X_1,...,X_n) whose first coordinate is sqrt(n) Xbar and whose remaining n-1 are independent N(0, sigma^2), with S^2 a function of only the latter; (c) geometry: Xbar is the projection onto the 1-vector, the residual vector is orthogonal, and orthogonal components of a spherical Gaussian are independent.
- **symbols:**
  - `Xbar _||_ S^2` — full statistical independence, not merely zero correlation
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## scaled_sample_variance_chi_squared

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An exact distributional identity. Proof: write sum ((X_i - mu)/sigma)^2 = sum ((X_i - Xbar)/sigma)^2 + ((Xbar - mu)/(sigma/sqrt n))^2. The left side is chi^2_n; the last term is chi^2_1 and (by the independence theorem) independent of the first; so the first is chi^2_{n-1} by the subtractive form of chi-squared additivity (Cochran).
- **symbols:**
  - `(n-1) S^2/sigma^2 = sum (X_i - Xbar)^2 / sigma^2` — a sum of squared standardized residuals
  - `n - 1` — df: n standardized observations minus 1 linear constraint (they are centered at Xbar)
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## t_statistic_distribution

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An exact sampling-distribution identity. Proof: sqrt(n)(Xbar - mu)/S = [ sqrt(n)(Xbar - mu)/sigma ] / [ S/sigma ] = Z / sqrt(V/(n-1)) with Z ~ N(0,1), V = (n-1)S^2/sigma^2 ~ chi^2_{n-1}, and Z _||_ V by normal_sample_mean_variance_independence. That is exactly the definition of t_{n-1}.
- **symbols:**
  - `sqrt(n)(Xbar - mu)/sigma ~ N(0,1)` — the numerator once sigma is put back
  - `S/sigma = sqrt( [(n-1)S^2/sigma^2] / (n-1) )` — sqrt of a chi^2_{n-1}/(n-1)
  - `n - 1` — the t degrees of freedom
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## cochran_theorem

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A quadratic-forms decomposition theorem for the spherical Gaussian. Uses linear_algebra_background (idempotent = projection, rank = trace for idempotents, simultaneous diagonalization). The 'sum of ranks = n' condition forces each A_j to be an orthogonal projection and the images to be orthogonal.
- **symbols:**
  - `A_j` — symmetric n x n matrices, type: real symmetric (projections onto orthogonal subspaces)
  - `r_j = rank(A_j) = tr(A_j)` — the df of the j-th component
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## one_way_anova_identity

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An exact algebraic (Pythagorean) identity -- the cross term vanishes because sum_j (X_ij - Xbar_i.) = 0 within each group. Under N(mu_i, sigma^2) errors and H0: all mu_i equal, Cochran makes SS_between/sigma^2 ~ chi^2_{k-1} independent of SS_within/sigma^2 ~ chi^2_{N-k}, so the F-ratio [SS_between/(k-1)] / [SS_within/(N-k)] ~ F_{k-1, N-k}.
- **symbols:**
  - `Xbar_i.` — the i-th group mean
  - `Xbar_..` — the grand mean
  - `SS_between / SS_within` — explained / unexplained variation
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## one_sample_t_test

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An exact-level test. The statistic is pivotal under the whole composite null {mu = mu_0, sigma^2 > 0} because its null law t_{n-1} does not involve sigma^2 -- so the size is alpha for every sigma^2.
- **symbols:**
  - `t = sqrt(n)(Xbar - mu_0)/S` — the t-statistic, ~ t_{n-1} under H0 (t_statistic_distribution)
  - `the one-sided version` — rejects when t > t_{n-1, 1-alpha}, UMP for H0: mu <= mu_0
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## two_sample_t_test

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** Exact under normality + equal variances (the pooled statistic is pivotal, ~ t_{n+m-2}). Welch's version is only approximately t-distributed (the Behrens-Fisher problem has no exact solution with a pivotal statistic).
- **symbols:**
  - `S_p^2 = [(n-1)S_X^2 + (m-1)S_Y^2] / (n+m-2)` — the pooled variance estimate
  - `Welch df` — nu = (S_X^2/n + S_Y^2/m)^2 / [ (S_X^2/n)^2/(n-1) + (S_Y^2/m)^2/(m-1) ]
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## f_test_equality_of_variances

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** Exact under normality (ratio of independent scaled chi-squares). Its Achilles heel: the F-test for variances is extremely sensitive to kurtosis -- far more than the t-test for means. Levene's and Brown-Forsythe tests are robust alternatives.
- **symbols:**
  - `the ratio of sample variances` — each S^2 scaled to a chi^2, the ratio is an F
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## confidence_set

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A frequentist coverage guarantee about the RANDOM set C(X), holding for all theta. A realized interval [l, u] either contains the fixed unknown theta or not -- the '95%' describes the long-run behaviour of the rule, not a probability about theta.
- **symbols:**
  - `C(X)` — the confidence set, type: random subset of Theta
  - `1 - alpha` — the confidence LEVEL (a property of the procedure, not of one realized interval)
  - `coverage` — theta -> P_theta(theta in C(X)), which should be >= 1 - alpha throughout

## coverage_probability

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A function of theta, the CI analogue of a test's power function. 'Exact' procedures have beta_C(theta) = 1 - alpha for all theta; discrete data usually force beta_C(theta) > 1 - alpha somewhere (conservative) and it oscillates in theta.
- **symbols:**
  - `beta_C(theta)` — coverage as a function of theta -- ideally flat at 1 - alpha

## pivotal_quantity

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A pivot mixes data and parameter in a way that cancels theta's effect on the distribution. It is the engine for exact confidence sets: bracket Q by its known quantiles, then solve for theta.
- **symbols:**
  - `Q(X, theta)` — the pivot -- involves theta, so it is NOT a statistic
  - `its (theta-free) distribution` — known, so its quantiles can be looked up
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## pivot_method

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An exact construction: coverage is 1 - alpha BY CONSTRUCTION because {theta in C(X)} = {a <= Q(X, theta) <= b} as events, and the latter has probability 1 - alpha for every theta (pivotality). The set is an interval iff Q is monotone in theta.
- **symbols:**
  - `a, b` — quantiles of the pivot's known distribution (e.g. +- t_{n-1, 1-alpha/2})
  - `'invert the pivot'` — solve the double inequality in theta
- **hypotheses:** pivotal_quantity

## confidence_set_test_duality

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An exact equivalence, holding at finite n. Proof: P_{theta_0}(theta_0 in C(X)) = P_{theta_0}(X in A(theta_0)) = 1 - (size of the test) >= 1 - alpha.
- **symbols:**
  - `the acceptance region A(theta_0) = { x : phi_{theta_0}(x) = 0 }` — and C(x) = { theta_0 : x in A(theta_0) } -- a 'horizontal slice' vs 'vertical slice' of the same set in (x, theta) space

## normal_mean_ci_known_variance

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** Exact. Invert the pivot sqrt(n)(Xbar - mu)/sigma ~ N(0,1). Width 2 z sigma/sqrt(n) is NON-random (sigma known) -- the only randomness is the center.
- **symbols:**

## normal_mean_ci_unknown_variance

- **status:** `well_formed`

- **symbols:**

## normal_variance_ci

- **status:** `well_formed`

## wald_interval

- **status:** `well_formed`

## delta_method_standard_error

- **status:** `well_formed`

## large_sample_wald_interval

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** The Wald interval specialized to the MLE, using mle_asymptotic_normality's variance I(theta_0)^{-1}. Asymptotic; shares the Wald interval's boundary weaknesses.
- **symbols:**

## null_hypothesis

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A subset of Theta, chosen before seeing the data. The test controls the probability of rejecting H0 when it is true; it does NOT control the probability of accepting a false H0. 'Fail to reject' is not 'accept'.
- **symbols:**
  - `Theta_0` — the null parameter set (a point for a 'simple' null, a set for a 'composite' null)
  - `'on trial'` — the asymmetry -- Type I error (wrongly rejecting H0) is controlled, Type II is not

## alternative_hypothesis

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A subset of Theta disjoint from (the closure of) Theta_0. The alternative is where POWER is measured; the choice of one- vs two-sided must be made before seeing the data.
- **symbols:**

  - `the direction of H1` — determines whether a one- or two-tailed test is used

## test_function

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A [0,1]-valued statistic. Randomization matters only for discrete data where no nonrandomized region has exactly the target size; in practice one reports the p-value instead.
- **symbols:**
  - `phi(x) = 1` — reject
  - `phi(x) = 0` — do not reject
  - `phi(x) in (0,1)` — reject with that probability (randomized -- needed for exact size on discrete data)

## size_of_test

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A supremum over the null set. 'Size' is the exact worst-case Type I rate; 'level' is any upper bound one is willing to claim. For a simple null Theta_0 = {theta_0} the sup is just E_{theta_0}[phi].
- **symbols:**
  - `E_theta[phi(X)] = P_theta(reject H0)` — the rejection probability at theta
  - `sup over Theta_0` — the worst-case Type I rate -- what 'level alpha' bounds

## power_function

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A single function on all of Theta. An ideal test has beta_phi <= alpha on Theta_0 and beta_phi close to 1 on Theta_1. Power increases with n, with the effect size, and with alpha; it decreases with variance.
- **symbols:**
  - `beta_phi(theta)` — one function giving both the Type I profile (on Theta_0) and the power (on Theta_1)
  - `power at a specific alternative theta_1` — beta_phi(theta_1) = 1 - P(Type II error at theta_1)
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## type_i_error

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** The two error kinds, treated asymmetrically: alpha is fixed by convention (0.05, 0.01, 5 sigma), power is then maximized. Which error is 'Type I' depends on which hypothesis is labelled the null -- a modelling choice reflecting which mistake is worse.
- **symbols:**
  - `alpha` — the tolerated Type I rate
  - `1 - beta_phi(theta_1)` — the Type II rate at alternative theta_1
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## neyman_pearson_lemma

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An optimality theorem, exact and finite-sample. Proof (sufficiency): for any other level-alpha test phi, the function (phi* - phi)(f1 - k f0) is >= 0 pointwise (check the two cases f1 > k f0 and f1 < k f0); integrate: E_{f1}[phi*] - E_{f1}[phi] >= k ( E_{f0}[phi*] - E_{f0}[phi] ) >= 0.
- **symbols:**
  - `f1/f0` — the likelihood ratio -- the optimal test statistic
  - `k` — the critical value chosen so the size is exactly alpha
  - `phi*` — the (possibly randomized) NP test
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## monotone_likelihood_ratio

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An ordering condition on the family. It is what upgrades 'most powerful for a simple alternative' (Neyman-Pearson) to 'UNIFORMLY most powerful for a one-sided composite alternative' (Karlin-Rubin).
- **symbols:**
  - `T(x)` — the statistic in which the ratio is monotone -- the natural sufficient statistic in an exponential family
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## karlin_rubin_theorem

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An optimality theorem for one-sided composite hypotheses. Proof: fix any theta_1 > theta_0; by Neyman-Pearson the MP test of theta_0 vs theta_1 rejects for large f(.; theta_1)/f(.; theta_0), which by MLR is 'large T' -- the same region {T > c} regardless of theta_1. Monotonicity of beta_phi handles the composite null.
- **symbols:**
  - `UMP` — uniformly most powerful -- the SAME test is most powerful against every alternative at once
  - `the size is attained at theta_0` — MLR makes beta_phi nondecreasing in theta, so sup over theta <= theta_0 is at theta_0
- **hypotheses:** monotone_likelihood_ratio

## likelihood_ratio_test

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A general recipe (works for composite hypotheses and nuisance parameters). The exact null distribution of Lambda is problem-specific; the value of the test is that -2 log Lambda -> chi^2_r generically (wilks_theorem).
- **symbols:**
  - `Lambda in [0,1]` — how much the best null fit is beaten by the best overall fit
  - `-2 log Lambda` — the deviance difference; its null distribution is asymptotically chi^2 (Wilks)
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## wilks_theorem

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An asymptotic null-distribution result -- it makes the LRT usable without deriving an exact null law. Proof: Taylor-expand ell around theta_hat and around theta_hat_0 (the constrained MLE); both expansions are quadratic forms in asymptotically-normal score vectors with covariance I; the difference is a chi^2_{d-r} by the projection/idempotency algebra.
- **symbols:**
  - `d - r = number of free parameters fixed by H0` — the chi^2 degrees of freedom
  - `Lambda` — the GLR statistic
- **hypotheses:** true_parameter_interior, fisher_information_positive_definite, log_likelihood_smooth

## wald_test

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An asymptotic test; the quadratic form of a standardized N(0, I^{-1}) vector is chi^2_d. Needs only the alternative-model fit -- convenient, but NOT invariant to reparametrization (W changes if you test log theta vs theta), its worst practical flaw.
- **symbols:**
  - `theta_hat` — the unrestricted MLE
  - `n I(theta_hat)` — estimated sample information (or observed information)
  - `W -> chi^2_d under H0` — by mle_asymptotic_normality + continuous mapping
- **hypotheses:** true_parameter_interior, fisher_information_positive_definite

## score_test

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An asymptotic test. Under H0, (1/sqrt n) U(theta_0) -> N(0, I(theta_0)) (score identity + information equality), so the standardized quadratic form -> chi^2_d. Invariant to reparametrization. Ideal when the alternative model is hard to fit (many nuisance parameters) or when scanning many candidate additions.
- **symbols:**
  - `U(theta_0)` — the score evaluated at the hypothesized value -- large in magnitude => theta_0 is a poor fit
  - `everything evaluated at theta_0` — the practical advantage: only fit the restricted model
- **hypotheses:** fisher_information_positive_definite

## three_tests_asymptotically_equivalent

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An asymptotic-equivalence statement. All three are, to first order, the same quadratic form in the asymptotically-normal score; they differ only in whether the information is evaluated at theta_hat (Wald), theta_0 (score), or effectively averaged (LRT), and in second-order terms.
- **symbols:**
  - `o_p(1)` — converges to 0 in probability
  - `local alternatives h/sqrt(n)` — the regime where power is non-degenerate (between alpha and 1)
- **hypotheses:** true_parameter_interior, fisher_information_positive_definite, log_likelihood_smooth

## p_value

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A [0,1]-valued statistic (a random variable before the data are seen). Under a simple continuous null it is Uniform(0,1) (p_value_uniform_under_null). Reject at level alpha iff p <= alpha. It is NOT P(H0 true | data) and NOT the probability the result is a fluke.
- **symbols:**
  - `T(x_obs)` — the observed test statistic
  - `P_theta(T >= T(x_obs))` — the null tail probability at least as extreme as observed
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## p_value_uniform_under_null

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An exact distributional fact, a direct corollary of the probability integral transform (F_0(T) ~ Uniform when T ~ F_0 continuous). It is what makes the p-value a calibrated evidence scale and lets p-values be combined (Fisher's method, etc.).
- **symbols:**
  - `p(X) = 1 - F_0(T(X))` — for an upper-tail test, F_0 the null CDF of T -- a probability integral transform
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## pearson_chi_squared_gof

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An asymptotic null-distribution result. X^2 is (asymptotically) the score statistic for the multinomial model, hence chi^2; it is also asymptotically equivalent to the LR statistic G^2 = 2 sum O_j log(O_j/E_j) (three_tests_asymptotically_equivalent).
- **symbols:**
  - `O_j` — observed count in cell j
  - `E_j = n p_j(theta_hat)` — expected count under the fitted null
  - `k - 1 - d` — cells, minus 1 (counts sum to n), minus d estimated parameters

## multiple_testing_fwer

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A joint (across the family) Type I error criterion. If each of m independent true nulls is tested at level alpha, P(at least one rejection) = 1 - (1 - alpha)^m approx m alpha -- so per-test alpha must shrink.
- **symbols:**
  - `m` — the number of tests in the family
  - `the family` — the set of hypotheses over which error is controlled -- a modelling choice
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## bonferroni_correction

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A finite-sample FWER bound. Proof: Boole's inequality (the union bound) -- FWER = P(union of false rejections) <= sum P(false rejection_i) <= m_0 (alpha/m) <= alpha, where m_0 <= m is the number of true nulls.
- **symbols:**
  - `alpha/m` — the per-comparison level
  - `equivalently` — reject H_{0,i} if its p-value p_i <= alpha/m; or adjust p_i -> min(1, m p_i)
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## benjamini_hochberg_fdr

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A weaker, more powerful error criterion than FWER: it tolerates a controlled FRACTION of false positives among the discoveries, which is the right notion for large-scale screening. FDR = FWER when all nulls are true; otherwise FDR <= FWER, so FDR procedures reject more.
- **symbols:**
  - `FDR` — expected fraction of rejections that are false (0 if no rejections)
  - `(k/m) alpha` — the rising BH threshold on the ordered p-values
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## empirical_cdf

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A statistic-valued function. For each fixed x, n F_hat_n(x) ~ Binomial(n, F(x)), so E[F_hat_n(x)] = F(x) (unbiased) and Var(F_hat_n(x)) = F(x)(1 - F(x))/n. As a process it is the basis of all distribution-free inference.
- **symbols:**
  - `F_hat_n` — the ECDF, a random right-continuous step function
  - `P_hat_n` — the empirical measure
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## order_statistic

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A statistic (permutation of the data). For a continuous parent, X_(k) has density n C(n-1, k-1) F(x)^{k-1} (1 - F(x))^{n-k} f(x); F(X_(k)) ~ Beta(k, n - k + 1).
- **symbols:**
  - `X_(k)` — the k-th smallest observation, type: statistic
  - `the full order statistic` — (X_(1),...,X_(n)) -- always sufficient, and minimal sufficient for a nonparametric family
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## sample_quantile

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A statistic (an order statistic, up to interpolation convention -- there are ~9 in common use). When F has positive density f(Q(p)) at the quantile, sqrt(n)(q_hat(p) - Q(p)) -> N(0, p(1-p) / f(Q(p))^2).

  - `Q(p) = F^{-1}(p)` — the population quantile being estimated
  - `the sample median` — p = 1/2

## glivenko_cantelli

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An almost-sure uniform-convergence statement, distribution-free. Proof: pointwise SLLN at a fixed grid of quantile levels, monotonicity of F and F_hat_n to fill in between grid points, then let the grid refine. It is the archetype of a 'uniform law of large numbers' over a class of sets (here, half-lines).

## dvoretzky_kiefer_wolfowitz

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A finite-sample, distribution-free concentration inequality for the sup-distance -- the quantitative (rate) form of Glivenko-Cantelli. It gives an honest confidence BAND for F, unlike pointwise CLT intervals.
- **symbols:**

  - `the bound is uniform in F` — distribution-free and finite-sample -- no asymptotics

## plug_in_estimator

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A construction. For a Hadamard-differentiable T, sqrt(n)(T(P_hat_n) - T(P)) -> N(0, E[IF(X)^2]) by the functional delta method, and E[IF^2]/n is the asymptotic variance (estimated by plugging in, or by the bootstrap / jackknife).
- **symbols:**

## bootstrap

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A resampling algorithm. Two error sources: the statistical approximation (P_hat_n for P) and the Monte Carlo approximation (B < inf). It works when T is a smooth functional of P (Hadamard-differentiable); it FAILS for non-smooth functionals (the max, a parameter on a boundary) and for statistics whose limit law depends on unknown nuisance parameters.

  - `T_n*` — the statistic recomputed on a resample
  - `B` — the number of Monte Carlo resamples (500-10000 typically)

## bootstrap_consistency

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An asymptotic conditional-convergence statement -- the theoretical justification for the bootstrap. The functional delta method for the bootstrap (van der Vaart-Wellner). For the SAMPLE MEAN and smooth functions of means it holds under a finite second moment.
- **symbols:**
  - `P*` — probability over the resampling, conditional on X_1..X_n
  - `IF` — the influence function of T at P

## kernel_density_estimator

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A statistic-valued function; a smoothed version of the empirical measure. It is biased for f (unlike the ECDF for F) -- density estimation is genuinely nonparametric, with a bias-variance tradeoff controlled by h (kde_bias_variance_tradeoff).

  - `h` — the bandwidth / smoothing parameter -- the crucial tuning knob
  - `f_hat_h` — a genuine density (integrates to 1) if K is

## kde_bias_variance_tradeoff

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An asymptotic rate statement -- the canonical nonparametric bias-variance tradeoff. Balancing h^4 (squared bias) against 1/(nh) (variance) gives h ~ n^{-1/5}. The n^{-4/5} rate is minimax-optimal over the class of twice-differentiable densities (matches the lower bound, minimax_rate).
- **symbols:**

## linear_model

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A structure. Key modelling assumptions, each a potential failure point: (1) linearity of E[y|X] in beta; (2) X fixed / exogenous (or inference is conditional on X); (3) full rank (no exact collinearity); (4) errors mean-zero, (5) homoskedastic, (6) uncorrelated, (7) Gaussian (needed only for EXACT small-sample inference, not for OLS's Gauss-Markov optimality). Uses linear_algebra_background throughout.
- **symbols:**
  - `X` — the design / model matrix, type: fixed real n x p matrix, rank p
  - `beta` — the coefficient vector, type: point of R^p (the parameter)
  - `eps` — the error vector, type: N(0, sigma^2 I_n) -- iid, mean 0, common variance, uncorrelated
  - `sigma^2` — the error variance (a second parameter)
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## ordinary_least_squares

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A definition via an optimization problem. The objective is strictly convex in b when X has full column rank, so the minimizer is unique and given by the normal equations. OLS = MLE under Gaussian errors; OLS = method of moments for the moment condition E[X eps] = 0.
- **symbols:**
  - `|| y - X b ||^2 = sum_i (y_i - x_i^T b)^2` — the least-squares criterion
  - `beta_hat = (X^T X)^{-1} X^T y` — the closed-form solution (normal_equations)
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## normal_equations

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** The stationarity condition for the least-squares objective: grad_b || y - X b ||^2 = -2 X^T (y - X b) = 0. An exact algebraic identity. Numerically one solves it via QR or Cholesky of X, not by forming (X^T X)^{-1}.
- **symbols:**
  - `X^T X` — the p x p Gram matrix, symmetric positive definite when rank(X) = p
  - `X^T y` — the p-vector of predictor-response inner products
  - `X^T (y - X beta_hat) = 0` — equivalently: the residual vector is orthogonal to every column of X
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## ols_is_projection

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A geometric statement: least squares IS orthogonal projection. Uses linear_algebra_background (projection matrices, idempotency, rank = trace for idempotents). H and I - H being complementary orthogonal projectors of ranks p and n - p is what drives every distributional result below (via Cochran).
- **symbols:**
  - `H` — the 'hat' matrix (it puts the hat on y), an orthogonal projector onto col(X)
  - `I - H` — the residual-maker / annihilator matrix, projector onto col(X)^perp, rank n - p
  - `h_ii` — the leverage of observation i -- its influence on its own fitted value; sum h_ii = p
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## residual_sum_of_squares

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A statistic. RSS is a quadratic form in y with the idempotent matrix I - H of rank n - p; under normal errors RSS/sigma^2 ~ chi^2_{n-p}, independent of beta_hat (Cochran), which is the engine of the t- and F-tests.
- **symbols:**
  - `e_i = y_i - x_i^T beta_hat` — the i-th residual (an estimate of eps_i, but with variance sigma^2(1 - h_ii) < sigma^2)
  - `RSS/sigma^2 ~ chi^2_{n-p}` — under the Gaussian linear model
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## unbiased_error_variance_estimator

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An exact identity, distribution-free (only E[eps]=0, Cov(eps) = sigma^2 I needed). Proof: E[RSS] = E[eps^T (I - H) eps] = sigma^2 tr(I - H) = sigma^2 (n - p). The regression generalization of bias_of_sample_variance (which is the p = 1, X = 1 case, giving n - 1).
- **symbols:**
  - `n - p` — the residual / error degrees of freedom -- observations minus fitted parameters
  - `the MLE (Gaussian) divides by n` — and is biased low by a factor (n-p)/n
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## gauss_markov_theorem

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An exact, finite-sample optimality result within the linear-unbiased class, requiring only first and second moments of eps. Proof: write any linear unbiased estimator as c^T beta_hat + d^T y with d^T X = 0 (unbiasedness); then Var = Var(c^T beta_hat) + sigma^2 ||d||^2 + 2 sigma^2 c^T (X^T X)^{-1} X^T d, and the cross term is 0 because X^T d = 0; so Var = Var(c^T beta_hat) + sigma^2 ||d||^2 >= Var(c^T beta_hat).
- **symbols:**
  - `BLUE` — Best (min variance) Linear Unbiased Estimator
  - `'linear'` — linear in the response y (a^T y for a fixed vector a)
  - `the competitor` — any a^T y with E[a^T y] = c^T beta for all beta
- **hypotheses:** unbiased_estimator

## ols_distribution_under_normal_errors

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An exact finite-sample distributional result. beta_hat = (X^T X)^{-1} X^T y is a fixed linear map of the Gaussian y, hence Gaussian; RSS is a quadratic form in (I - H) y; H y (which determines beta_hat) and (I - H) y are orthogonal linear images of a spherical Gaussian, hence independent (Cochran).
- **symbols:**
  - `sigma^2 (X^T X)^{-1}` — the exact covariance of beta_hat; its j-th diagonal is Var(beta_hat_j)
  - `beta_hat _||_ RSS` — the multivariate analogue of Xbar _||_ S^2, from H and I - H being orthogonal projectors
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## coefficient_t_test

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** Exact under the Gaussian linear model. The numerator ~ N(0, sigma^2 (X^TX)^{-1}_jj) is independent of sigma_hat^2, and (n-p)sigma_hat^2/sigma^2 ~ chi^2_{n-p}, so the ratio is exactly t_{n-p} (assemble the t definition).
- **symbols:**
  - `se(beta_hat_j) = sigma_hat sqrt((X^T X)^{-1}_jj)` — the reported standard error
  - `t_{n-p}` — n - p residual degrees of freedom
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## overall_f_test

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** Exact under normality. RSS_0 - RSS and RSS are, after dividing by sigma^2, independent chi^2_q and chi^2_{n-p} (Cochran: the restriction subspace and the full residual space are orthogonal), so their scaled ratio is F_{q, n-p}. It is the LRT for the linear restriction (special_case_of likelihood_ratio_test).
- **symbols:**
  - `RSS_0` — residual SS of the reduced (restricted) model
  - `RSS` — residual SS of the full model
  - `q` — number of restrictions (numerator df)
  - `n - p` — full-model error df (denominator df)
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## r_squared

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A descriptive statistic in [0,1] (for a model with an intercept). NOT a goodness-of-fit test, NOT a measure of correct specification, and NOT comparable across different response variables or transformations. R^2 never decreases when a predictor is added -- hence adjusted R^2.
- **symbols:**
  - `TSS` — total sum of squares (RSS of the intercept-only model)
  - `adjusted R^2 = 1 - (RSS/(n-p)) / (TSS/(n-1))` — penalizes added predictors
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## partitioned_regression

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An exact algebraic identity. It formalizes 'controlling for X_1': a multiple-regression coefficient is a SIMPLE regression coefficient on the parts of y and x_2 that X_1 cannot explain. Proof: block normal equations, solve the X_1 block, substitute.
- **symbols:**
  - `M_1 = I - X_1(X_1^T X_1)^{-1} X_1^T` — the residual-maker for X_1
  - `'partialling out' X_1` — regressing both y and X_2 on X_1 and taking residuals
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## likelihood_principle

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A strong, contested normative principle. Its bite: the stopping rule of a sequential experiment is irrelevant to the evidence (only the observed likelihood matters), which conflicts with frequentist error control. Birnbaum's derivation from two mild-seeming principles is the surprise.
- **symbols:**
  - `proportional` — L(theta; x_1) = c(x_1, x_2) L(theta; x_2) for all theta, with c > 0 free of theta
  - `the tension` — Bayesian and pure-likelihood methods obey it; frequentist methods using tail areas (p-values, confidence intervals, unbiasedness) generally VIOLATE it
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## conditionality_principle

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** A normative principle. It says the relevant reference set for a frequentist calculation is 'repetitions of the experiment I actually did', not 'repetitions of the random choice of experiment'. Widely (not universally) accepted.
- **symbols:**
  - `a mixture experiment` — flip a coin; heads -> measure with the precise instrument, tails -> the crude one
  - `the ancillary statistic` — which experiment was chosen -- its law is free of theta

## le_cam_lan_theory

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** The organizing theorem of modern asymptotic statistics (Le Cam). It says all regular parametric models look locally like a Gaussian location problem, so optimality questions (efficient estimation, most powerful testing) are answered ONCE, in the Gaussian shift experiment, and transferred back. Boundary node: stated, cited.
- **symbols:**
  - `h` — the local parameter (a direction and magnitude of a 1/sqrt(n) perturbation)
  - `Delta_n = (1/sqrt n) sum score(X_i; theta_0)` — the normalized score, the LAN 'central sequence'
  - `the limit experiment` — observing one draw from N(h, I(theta_0)^{-1}) -- everything asymptotic reduces to this
- **hypotheses:** log_likelihood_smooth, fisher_information_positive_definite, true_parameter_interior

## hajek_convolution_theorem

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** The rigorous, estimator-free statement of 'you cannot beat the Fisher-information bound' -- replacing the CRLB's unbiasedness assumption with regularity. Boundary node: stated, cited. It is why 'asymptotically efficient' = 'asymptotic variance I^{-1}' is the right definition.
- **symbols:**
  - `regular` — the local limit law is the same for every local parameter h (rules out Hodges superefficiency)
  - `* M` — an extra independent 'noise' term -- so the efficient estimator's limit is a lower bound in the convolution order
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## local_asymptotic_minimax

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** The strongest and cleanest optimality statement in the classical theory: no regularity on the estimator at all, and it rules out ALL superefficiency (Hodges' estimator has local minimax risk that BLOWS UP as c grows). Boundary node: stated, cited.
- **symbols:**
  - `bowl-shaped loss` — l(x) = l(-x), sublevel sets convex (squared error, absolute error, 0-1)
  - `the local minimax over |h| <= c` — shrinking neighbourhoods, then c -> inf
- **hypotheses:** (none / unconditional within scope)

## donsker_theorem

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** The process-level strengthening of the pointwise CLT for F_hat_n, and the rate-level strengthening of Glivenko-Cantelli. It is the analytic engine behind the limiting null distributions of the Kolmogorov-Smirnov and Cramer-von Mises statistics, and behind the functional delta method (hence bootstrap consistency for smooth functionals). Boundary node: stated, cited.
- **symbols:**

  - `G` — the limiting Gaussian process (Brownian bridge composed with F)
  - `a Donsker class` — a function class over which this uniform CLT holds (finite uniform entropy / bracketing)

## minimax_rate

- **status:** `well_formed`
- **note:** An asymptotic minimax RATE statement, with matching upper (a constructive estimator) and lower (Fano / Assouad / Le Cam two-point) bounds. It quantifies 'nonparametric problems need more data' and pins the price of dimension (the curse: rate collapses as d grows) and the reward of smoothness. Boundary node: stated, cited.

  - `d` — the dimension of the function's domain
  - `the exponent 2 beta/(2 beta + d)` — < 1, approaching 1 as beta -> inf (smoother => closer to parametric rate)
